For each region, this file lists what makes it distinct in every category and where the major *internal* divides fall. These profiles serve as the **regional priors**: what the county scores in [`PARAMETERS.md`](PARAMETERS.md) should broadly confirm, and what the C4 regional-coherence check measures against.

## 1. New England & Mid-Atlantic (The Northeast)

**Sub-regions:** New England (ME, VT, NH, MA, RI, CT) · Mid-Atlantic (NY, PA, NJ)

### History
- **Colonial (highest era scores in the nation):** Plymouth (1620), Massachusetts Bay, New Amsterdam (1624), New Sweden on the Delaware (1638), Penn's Pennsylvania (1681). Colonial street plans, town greens and churches survive in large numbers, which pushes the NRHP term up.
- **Founding years:** Lexington and Concord, Saratoga, Trenton, Valley Forge. Philadelphia and New York both served as national capitals.
- **Antebellum:** The Erie Canal (1825) built the upstate NY corridor (Albany → Buffalo). Textile mill towns appeared (Lowell, Fall River). New England was the hub of abolitionism. Gettysburg (Adams Co., PA).
- **Industrial (peak era for the Mid-Atlantic):** Pittsburgh steel, anthracite coal (Scranton/Wilkes-Barre), Bethlehem Steel, immigration through Ellis Island, garment and port economy.
- **Postwar:** Levittown-style suburbanization (Nassau, Bucks), then deindustrialization starts.
- **Reagan era:** Rust Belt collapse in western PA and upstate NY, alongside a finance boom in NYC and Fairfield County, CT.
- **Tech boom / Modern:** Boston–Cambridge biotech, NYC tech and finance, Marcellus Shale gas in PA (2008+).
- **Projections:** Slow or negative growth in rural New England, upstate NY and northern/western PA, with the oldest populations in the US (Maine has the highest median age). Moderate metro growth. A possible net receiver of climate migration.
- **Internal divide:** New England = Colonial-dominant · Mid-Atlantic interior = Industrial-dominant.

### Jobs / main unique positions
- **Centers of industry:** global finance (New York Co.; Fairfield CT hedge funds), insurance (Hartford), pharma (central NJ), biotech (Middlesex MA), higher education and hospitals ("eds and meds": Boston, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Rochester).
- **Specializations:** commercial fishing (New Bedford, one of the top US ports by catch value; Gloucester), lobster (Maine), naval shipbuilding (Bath Iron Works ME; Electric Boat in Groton CT), dairy and maple (Vermont), tourism (Cape Cod, the Adirondacks, the Poconos).
- **Historical differentiators:** textile mills (Merrimack Valley), steel (Mon Valley), anthracite and bituminous coal (PA), paper mills (Maine), Kodak and Xerox (Rochester), GE (Schenectady), IBM (Hudson Valley).

### Linguistic regions (narrow → general)
- **Eastern New England:** non-rhotic ("pahk the cah"), *wicked*, Mary/marry/merry kept distinct. Boston merges cot–caught; Rhode Island keeps them distinct. Maine "Downeast" speech.
- **Western New England:** rhotic, closer to Inland North.
- **New York City:** traditionally non-rhotic, raised "cawfee" vowel, split short-a, *youse*, "on line" (for "in line").
- **Philadelphia:** "wooder", *youse*, *hoagie*, *jawn*, its own short-a system.
- **Western Pennsylvania:** *yinz*, "dahntahn", "the car needs washed", cot–caught merged.
- **Inland North (Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse):** Northern Cities Vowel Shift.
- **Heritage:** Pennsylvania Dutch (Lancaster County), Hasidic Yiddish (Kings, Rockland and Orange NY; Ocean NJ).
- **General:** L1 = North, except W. PA and Philadelphia = Midland. Soft drink: *soda*, except *pop* in W. PA and Buffalo.

### Religious views
- **Most Catholic region** (RI, MA, NJ, CT and NY metro are the highest-share states).
- **Largest Jewish population** (NYC metro; Orthodox concentrations in Brooklyn, Rockland, Orange, Ocean NJ).
- **New England is the least religious part of the East** (VT, NH and ME rank near the bottom nationally in religiosity) and has a Congregationalist/UCC heritage.
- **Pennsylvania:** Quaker founding legacy; Amish and Mennonites (Lancaster); Lutheran and Reformed in the German "Dutch Country".
- Muslim, Hindu and Sikh communities in NJ and the NYC suburbs; Black Protestant concentrations in Philadelphia, Newark and Harlem.

### Environment
- **Appalachian system:** White, Green, Berkshire, Catskill and Pocono mountains, plus the Allegheny Plateau. The Adirondacks are a geologically separate dome.
- **Coastal Plain / glacial landforms:** Cape Cod, Long Island (glacial moraines), NJ Pine Barrens.
- **Great Lakes shores:** Lake Erie (Erie PA, Buffalo) and Lake Ontario.
- **The BosWash megalopolis** (the densest corridor in the US) sits next to very rural northern NE. Maine is the most forested state by share.
- Entirely east of the Mississippi. Mostly glaciated. Humid continental climate. "Fall line" cities (Trenton, Philadelphia) sit where the Piedmont meets the coastal plain.

### Voting history
- **Yankee Republican stronghold 1860–1960s.** Vermont voted Republican every presidential election from 1856 to 1988 except 1964.
- **Realigned to Democratic from 1992.** MA, VT, RI, NYC and the NJ urban counties are now among the most Democratic places in the US.
- **Internal divides:** the rural "T" of central PA is deep R. Upstate rural NY is R. NH is a swing state. Maine splits its electoral votes (ME-2). PA is the decisive swing state.
- **Recent swings:** Obama→Trump shifts in old mill and coal counties (Luzerne PA, Erie PA). A rightward swing in NYC and NJ Hispanic and Asian neighborhoods in 2024.

## 2. The Southeast

**Sub-regions:** South Atlantic (DE, MD, DC, VA, WV, NC, SC, GA, FL) · East South Central (KY, TN, MS, AL) · West South Central / Cajun-Delta (LA, AR)

### History
- **Colonial:** St. Augustine, FL (1565, the oldest continuously inhabited European settlement in the US), Jamestown (1607), Maryland (1634, a Catholic refuge), Charleston (1670), Georgia (1733). The plantation system and slavery took root.
- **Founding years:** The Virginia dynasty (Washington, Jefferson, Madison, Monroe), Yorktown, the creation of DC (1790).
- **Antebellum/Civil War (peak era for the Deep South):** Cotton Kingdom expansion into AL, MS and LA. Indian Removal (Trail of Tears, 1830s). The Confederacy. Most Civil War battlefields are in VA, TN and GA.
- **Industrial:** The "New South": Piedmont textile mills, tobacco (Durham, Winston-Salem), Birmingham steel, sharecropping, Jim Crow. The Great Migration begins (~1910).
- **Roaring 20s:** Florida land boom and bust (1926).
- **Depression:** The TVA (1933) transformed the Tennessee Valley.
- **Postwar & Civil Rights (critical era):** Montgomery, Birmingham, Selma, Greensboro. Military bases, NASA (Huntsville, Cape Canaveral), air conditioning and the start of Sun Belt growth.
- **Reagan era:** Atlanta's rise and the first foreign auto plants (Nissan, Smyrna TN, 1983).
- **Tech boom:** Research Triangle, Northern Virginia (the early commercial internet), Charlotte banking.
- **Modern:** Large in-migration to FL, the Carolinas and TN. Northern Virginia becomes the world's largest data-center market (Loudoun). An EV and battery manufacturing belt (GA, TN, KY, NC). Delta, Black Belt and coal counties depopulate.
- **Projections:** FL, NC, SC, TN and GA lead projected growth, but carry the highest sea-level and hurricane exposure (Miami-Dade, Hampton Roads, coastal Louisiana land loss). Appalachian coal and Delta counties continue to decline.

### Jobs / main unique positions
- **Centers of industry:** federal government and contractors (DC, NoVA, MD), military (Norfolk, the largest naval base; Fort Bragg NC; Fort Campbell KY), banking (Charlotte), logistics hubs (Memphis/FedEx, Louisville/UPS, Atlanta airport), aerospace (Huntsville, Charleston), healthcare corporations (Nashville).
- **Specializations:** foreign-owned auto plants (BMW in Spartanburg, Mercedes in Vance AL, Toyota in Georgetown KY, Hyundai in Montgomery), poultry (north GA, AR, AL, MS), carpets (Whitfield GA), Walmart/Tyson/J.B. Hunt (NW Arkansas), petrochemicals (Louisiana's Baton Rouge–New Orleans river corridor), offshore oil support (Terrebonne, Lafourche), bourbon and thoroughbreds (Kentucky), Delta rice/cotton/soy, timber and paper (MS, AL, GA), tourism and retirement (Orlando, Myrtle Beach, Gatlinburg, the Florida coasts).
- **Historical differentiators:** tobacco (KY, NC), textiles (Carolina Piedmont), furniture (High Point NC), Appalachian coal (WV, eastern KY, southwest VA), cotton plantations (Black Belt, Delta).

### Linguistic regions (narrow → general)
- **Southern American English (L1 South):** pin–pen merger, "rahd" (/ay/ monophthong), Southern Vowel Shift, *y'all*, *fixin' to*, *might could*.
- **Coastal / Lowland South vs Inland / Upland South:** Charleston and Savannah speech differ from the inland Southern Shift; older coastal speakers are non-rhotic.
- **Appalachian English:** *you'uns*, a-prefixing ("a-huntin'"), strongly rhotic.
- **Gullah Geechee:** an English-based creole of the Sea Islands (SC/GA coast).
- **Cajun English and Louisiana French** in the Acadiana parishes. New Orleans "Yat" sounds closer to NYC than to the South.
- **Outer Banks / Ocracoke "Hoi Toider"** and Tangier Island, VA.
- **Baltimore ("Bawlmer", *hon*)** follows Mid-Atlantic patterns.
- **Non-Southern islands:** DC/NoVA, and South and Central Florida, where ANAE classes Miami and Orlando as not Southern. Miami English shows Spanish contact.
- AAVE is strongest across the Black Belt and in urban centers. Soft drink: *coke* as the generic term.

### Religious views
- **The Bible Belt core:** the highest Evangelical shares in the country, with the Southern Baptist Convention dominant in TN, AL, MS, KY, GA, SC and AR. MS and AL rank as the most religious states.
- **Highest Black Protestant shares** (National Baptist, AME, and COGIC, founded in Memphis) across the Black Belt and the Delta.
- **Catholic exceptions:** south Louisiana (Acadiana and New Orleans, majority Catholic), South Florida (Hispanic Catholic), Maryland.
- **Jewish:** Palm Beach and Broward have large retiree communities.
- Pentecostal and Holiness origins in Appalachia (Church of God, Cleveland TN). Methodist strength in the Upper South.

### Environment
- **Physiographic sequence:** Atlantic and Gulf Coastal Plain → Piedmont → Blue Ridge → Ridge-and-Valley → Appalachian/Cumberland Plateau. Fall-line cities: Richmond, Raleigh, Columbia, Augusta, Macon, Columbus GA.
- **Mississippi Alluvial Plain (the Delta),** Ozark and Ouachita mountains (AR), the Atchafalaya Basin, the Everglades and Florida karst, the Chesapeake Bay, barrier islands.
- **The Black Belt:** a crescent of dark prairie soil across AL and MS, the origin of the name.
- **Mississippi River:** AR and most of LA are west; the Florida Parishes are east; several river parishes straddle.
- Humid subtropical. The highest hurricane exposure. Heavily rural overall but with fast-growing metros (Atlanta, Charlotte, Nashville, Raleigh, Orlando).

### Voting history
- **The "Solid South":** Democratic from 1876 to the 1940s. Then the 1948 Dixiecrats, Goldwater carrying the Deep South in 1964, and Nixon's Southern Strategy. The region realigned to Republican at the presidential level by the 1980s–2000s and at the state level by the 2010s.
- **Black Belt counties remain strongly Democratic.** Appalachia went from coal-Democrat (WV and KY into the 1990s) to the most Republican area in the nation.
- **Mountain Republicans:** East Tennessee and western North Carolina have voted Republican since the Civil War (Unionist heritage).
- **Swing / trend:** VA has become lean D (NoVA). GA and NC are swing states (Atlanta, Raleigh, Charlotte suburbs). FL has shifted sharply R since 2016; Miami-Dade voted R in 2024 for the first time since 1988.

## 3. The Midwest

**Sub-regions:** East North Central / Great Lakes (OH, IN, IL, MI, WI) · West North Central / Great Plains (MN, IA, MO, ND, SD, NE, KS)

### History
- **Colonial:** French, not English: Detroit (1701), Kaskaskia, Vincennes, Ste. Genevieve, St. Louis (1764), Green Bay, Prairie du Chien. Most of the region was Native land.
- **Founding years:** The Northwest Ordinance (1787) banned slavery north of the Ohio River and set up the township-and-range survey grid that still shapes roads and county lines. Ohio statehood (1803). The Louisiana Purchase (1803) took in everything west of the Mississippi.
- **Antebellum:** Canals and railroads make Chicago the national hub. Mass German, Irish and Scandinavian settlement. The Missouri Compromise (1820), Bleeding Kansas, the Underground Railroad, the Homestead Act (1862).
- **Industrial (peak era):** Chicago meatpacking, Gary steel, Akron rubber, Minneapolis flour, the Iron Range, Detroit's auto industry (Ford's assembly line, 1913), homesteading of the Plains.
- **Depression:** The Dust Bowl (KS, NE, the Dakotas) and the Flint sit-down strike (1936–37).
- **Postwar:** Manufacturing peaks. The Great Migration reshapes Chicago, Detroit and Cleveland.
- **Reagan era (decline):** The 1980s farm crisis and Rust Belt deindustrialization (Youngstown, Flint, Gary).
- **Modern:** The Bakken oil boom (ND, 2008–2015). Columbus, Indianapolis, Des Moines, Sioux Falls and Omaha grow. Detroit goes through bankruptcy (2013) and recovery.
- **Projections:** Continued depopulation of the rural Plains and small industrial towns. Metro hubs grow. Great Lakes water is a long-run advantage as a climate refuge.

### Jobs / main unique positions
- **Centers of industry:** autos (Wayne, Oakland, Macomb MI; Toledo; the Indiana auto corridor), steel (Lake County IN), RVs (Elkhart IN builds most US RVs), commodity and futures trading (Chicago), insurance (Des Moines, Omaha, Columbus, Bloomington IL), medical devices and Mayo Clinic (the Twin Cities; Rochester MN), aircraft (Wichita).
- **Specializations:** corn, soy and ethanol (IA, IL, NE, MN), meatpacking towns (Sioux City, Storm Lake, Worthington), Wisconsin dairy and paper, agribusiness headquarters (Cargill, ADM, Deere), taconite (Iron Range), Bakken oil (McKenzie and Williams ND), the Minot ICBM base (ND).
- **Historical differentiators:** auto, steel and rubber (deep legacy, low persistence in Flint, Youngstown and Gary), Grand Rapids furniture, Milwaukee brewing, Battle Creek cereal, Iron Range mining, Peoria (Caterpillar).
- The USDA "farming-dependent" county type is concentrated in the Plains states.

### Linguistic regions (narrow → general)
- **Inland North:** the Northern Cities Vowel Shift (Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland, Toledo, Grand Rapids).
- **North Central / Upper Midwest:** monophthongal "boat"/"bait", *ope*, *you betcha*, Canadian raising, a Scandinavian and German substrate (MN, WI, the Dakotas).
- **Yooper (Upper Peninsula, MI):** Finnish influence, *eh*.
- **Midland (central OH, IN, IL, MO, KS, NE):** *positive anymore*, "needs washed", fronted /o/. The closest thing to "General American".
- **Southern influence:** "Little Egypt" in southern IL, southern IN, the Missouri Bootheel, the Ozarks.
- **St. Louis:** an older "farty-farty" (*forty-forty*) vowel and *soda* in a *pop* region.
- **Heritage and immigrant languages:** Pennsylvania Dutch (Holmes OH, LaGrange IN), Arabic (Dearborn), Hmong and Somali (Twin Cities), Lakota (Oglala Lakota County SD), Spanish (meatpacking towns).
- Soft drink: *pop*.

### Religious views
- **Lutheran heartland:** ELCA and Missouri Synod Lutherans in MN, ND, SD, WI and IA (Scandinavian and German heritage).
- **Catholic strength:** WI, IL, eastern ND, Dubuque IA, Stearns MN and the Ohio cities.
- **Methodist belt** across central IA, KS and NE.
- **Dutch Reformed:** West Michigan (Holland, Grand Rapids) and Sioux County IA, one of the highest adherence rates in the nation.
- **Anabaptist:** Amish and Mennonites in Holmes OH, Elkhart and LaGrange IN, and Kansas.
- **Evangelical** in southern IN, IL and MO (Springfield MO is the Assemblies of God headquarters).
- Black Protestant concentrations in Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland and St. Louis. Muslim community in Dearborn.

### Environment
- **Interior Lowlands and Great Plains,** divided by the **100th meridian**: humid farming to the east, semi-arid ranching to the west, within ND, SD, NE and KS.
- **Great Lakes:** Michigan touches four of the five lakes. The **Driftless Area** (SW Wisconsin) escaped glaciation, unlike its neighbors. The Red River Valley is a former glacial lakebed. Also the NE Sandhills, the Black Hills and Badlands (SD), and the Ozark Plateau (MO).
- **Mississippi River:** OH, IN, IL, MI and WI are east; IA, MO, ND, SD, NE and KS are west; MN contains the headwaters and straddles.
- Extreme continental climate and Tornado Alley. Some of the least dense counties east of the Rockies (frontier counties in NE, SD, ND and KS).

### Voting history
- **The Republican Party was founded here** (Ripon WI, 1854), and the region was a Republican heartland for a century.
- **Progressive and populist strains:** La Follette (WI), Minnesota Farmer-Labor, the ND Nonpartisan League.
- **The swing-state core:** MI and WI. Ohio was a bellwether from 1964 to 2016 and is now lean R. Vigo County IN's bellwether streak broke in 2020.
- **Obama→Trump counties are concentrated here** (Iowa, the WI Driftless Area, the MN Iron Range).
- The Plains are deep R. Cook, Wayne, Hennepin and Dane are strongly D. Minnesota has the longest D presidential streak (since 1976).

## 4. The Southwest

**Core states:** TX, OK, NM, AZ. (East Texas and southeastern Oklahoma carry a Southeast secondary affinity.)

### History
- **Colonial (Spanish):** Santa Fe (1610, the oldest state capital), the Pueblo Revolt (1680), the San Antonio missions (1718+), the Tucson presidio (1775), El Camino Real. Pueblo, Navajo, Apache and Comanche nations. No English colonial layer.
- **Founding years:** New Spain, then Mexico (1821). A low US imprint.
- **Antebellum/Civil War:** The Texas Revolution and Republic (1836), annexation (1845), the Mexican-American War and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), the Gadsden Purchase (1853). The Five Tribes were removed to Indian Territory (Oklahoma).
- **Industrial:** Cattle drives (the Chisholm Trail), railroads, the Oklahoma land runs (1889), Spindletop oil (1901), AZ copper towns (Bisbee, Jerome, Morenci). OK, NM and AZ reached statehood in 1907 and 1912.
- **Roaring 20s:** The Oklahoma/Texas oil boom (Tulsa as "Oil Capital") and the Tulsa Race Massacre (1921).
- **Depression:** The **Dust Bowl epicenter** (the OK and TX Panhandles) and the "Okie" exodus.
- **Postwar:** The Manhattan Project (Los Alamos, the Trinity test), White Sands, NASA Houston, air-conditioned Sun Belt growth (Phoenix), the Bracero program.
- **Reagan era:** The 1980s oil bust (Houston, Oklahoma) and the S&L crisis.
- **Tech boom:** Austin, the Dallas Telecom Corridor, Intel in Chandler and Rio Rancho, and NAFTA border trade (Laredo).
- **Modern:** The shale revolution (the Permian Basin: Midland/Odessa; the Eagle Ford), the Texas Triangle mega-growth, Phoenix semiconductors (TSMC), Tesla in Austin, SpaceX at Boca Chica.
- **Projections:** Among the fastest-growing metros (DFW, Houston, Austin, San Antonio, Phoenix), constrained by Colorado River shortages, Ogallala Aquifer depletion and extreme heat.

### Jobs / main unique positions
- **Centers of industry:** oil and gas (the Permian; Houston as the energy capital; Tulsa and OKC), petrochemicals and refining (Harris, Jefferson TX), copper (Arizona produces most US copper), national labs (Los Alamos, Sandia), border trade (Laredo, the busiest land port), semiconductors (Maricopa, Austin, Sandoval NM).
- **Specializations:** cattle ranching and feedlots (TX Panhandle), cotton (Lubbock and the High Plains), winter vegetables (Yuma), military (Fort Hood, Fort Bliss, JBSA, Tinker, Kirtland, Luke, Davis-Monthan), aerospace (NASA JSC; Lockheed in Fort Worth), wind power (West TX, OK), tribal government and gaming (the Navajo Nation; Cherokee Nation in Tahlequah), tourism (the Grand Canyon, Sedona, Santa Fe), retirement (Sun City).
- **Historical differentiators:** oil boomtowns, Dust Bowl farming, copper camps, cattle trails.

### Linguistic regions (narrow → general)
- **Texas South:** Southern features (*y'all*, *fixin' to*, pin–pen merger). A West Texas / High Plains twang.
- **Oklahoma:** a Southern–Midland transition zone.
- **Chicano English and Tejano Spanish:** in the Rio Grande Valley and border counties (Starr, Webb, Zapata, Hidalgo), the large majority speak Spanish at home.
- **New Mexican Spanish:** a distinct variety descended from 17th-century colonists (northern NM).
- **Indigenous languages:** Navajo, the most-spoken Native American language in the US (Apache, Navajo and McKinley counties). Also Tohono Oʼodham, Hopi, Apache, Zuni and Keresan, and Cherokee and other tribal-language revival in Oklahoma.
- **European heritage:** Texas German (Hill Country: Fredericksburg, New Braunfels) and Texas Czech.
- **Leveling:** Phoenix and the Texas metro cores are moving toward General American / Western speech through migration.

### Religious views
- **Hispanic Catholic dominance** in South Texas and New Mexico (NM has the highest Catholic share in the West). The Penitente tradition persists in northern NM.
- **Southern Baptist / Evangelical dominance** in Oklahoma (among the most Evangelical states) and in North, East and West Texas. **Churches of Christ** are unusually strong (Abilene, West TX).
- **Latter-day Saints** in eastern Arizona (Mesa; Navajo, Apache and Graham counties).
- Native traditional religion and the Native American Church. Megachurches (Houston, Dallas). Rapid Hindu and Muslim growth in suburbs (Fort Bend, Collin).

### Environment
- **Deserts:** Chihuahuan (West TX, southern NM), Sonoran (southern AZ), Mojave (NW AZ).
- **Plateaus and ranges:** the Colorado Plateau (the Grand Canyon, the Four Corners), the Basin and Range, the Sangre de Cristo Mountains (the southern Rockies).
- **Plains:** the Llano Estacado / High Plains caprock and the Edwards Plateau / Hill Country, cut off by the **Balcones Escarpment** (the Austin–San Antonio line).
- **East:** the Gulf Coastal Plain and the East Texas Piney Woods.
- **A steep rainfall gradient:** roughly 50+ inches a year in East Texas and under 10 in El Paso. The 100th meridian crosses TX and OK.
- Entirely west of the Mississippi. The largest reservation (the Navajo Nation) and Oklahoma's post-*McGirt* (2020) tribal jurisdictions. Extreme county-size contrasts: Maricopa is one of the most populous counties in the US, and Loving TX is the least populous.

### Voting history
- **Texas:** Democratic through the 1970s, Republican-dominant since the 1990s. Metro cores (Travis, Dallas, Harris, Bexar) are D; suburbs are competitive.
- **Oklahoma:** every county has voted Republican in every presidential election since 2004.
- **South Texas border:** historically deep D, with sharp Republican shifts in 2020–2024. Zapata flipped in 2020; Starr flipped in 2024.
- **Arizona:** Goldwater Republican, now the swing state (Maricopa decides). Navajo Nation areas are strongly D.
- **New Mexico:** lean D. The Hispano north is D; the oil-patch southeast ("Little Texas") is R.

## 5. The Mountain West

**Interior West:** CO, WY, MT, ID, UT, NV. (North Idaho has a Pacific NW affinity; the eastern plains of CO, MT and WY have a Midwest affinity.)

### History
- **Colonial:** Mostly Native land (Ute, Shoshone, Crow, Blackfeet, Lakota, Cheyenne, Arapaho, Paiute, Nez Perce). Spanish exploration (Domínguez–Escalante, 1776). Hispano settlement of the San Luis Valley (San Luis, CO, 1851, the oldest town in Colorado).
- **Founding years:** Lewis and Clark (MT, ID, 1805).
- **Antebellum:** The fur trade and mountain men. The Oregon Trail through WY and ID. **Mormon settlement of Salt Lake (1847)**. The Pike's Peak gold rush (1858–59; Denver). The Comstock Lode (1859; Virginia City NV). Nevada statehood (1864).
- **Industrial (peak era):** The transcontinental railroad (Promontory UT, 1869). Mining booms: Butte copper, Leadville silver, Coeur d'Alene silver. Cattle empires. Little Bighorn (1876). Yellowstone (1872, the first national park). Wyoming women's suffrage (1869).
- **Depression:** Hoover Dam (1931–36), legalized gambling in Nevada (1931), the CCC.
- **Postwar:** The Nevada Test Site (1951), the Las Vegas Strip, the uranium boom (Colorado Plateau), NORAD, ski resorts (Aspen, Vail).
- **Reagan era:** The Sagebrush Rebellion over federal land. The energy bust (oil shale's "Black Sunday", 1982). The Butte Anaconda mine closure (1983).
- **Tech boom:** Boulder/Denver telecom, early Utah tech, Boise (Micron, HP), the Las Vegas megaresorts.
- **Modern:** Utah and Idaho are among the fastest-growing states. "Zoom towns" (Bozeman, Jackson, Coeur d'Alene) and housing crises in resort counties. Powder River coal declines.
- **Projections:** High growth on the Wasatch Front, the Front Range, Boise and Las Vegas, constrained by water (Colorado River, Lake Mead) and wildfire.

### Jobs / main unique positions
- **Centers of industry:** coal (the Powder River Basin, about 40% of US coal; Campbell WY), trona (Sweetwater WY, the world's largest deposit), gold (Elko and the Carlin Trend; Nevada is the top US producer), copper (Bingham Canyon UT), gaming (Clark, Washoe), outdoor recreation and ski resorts.
- **Specializations:** ski and amenity counties (Pitkin, Summit, Eagle, San Miguel, Routt CO; Teton WY, which has the highest per-capita income in the US; Blaine ID; Summit UT; Gallatin MT), Idaho potatoes and Magic Valley dairy, sugar beets, cattle and sheep, oil and gas (Weld CO; the Uinta Basin UT), lithium (Thacker Pass NV).
- **Federal:** Idaho National Laboratory, NREL, NIST/NOAA Boulder, Hill AFB, the Air Force Academy, Space Command (Colorado Springs), the Nevada National Security Site, ICBM fields (Malmstrom MT, F.E. Warren WY).
- **Tech:** "Silicon Slopes" (Utah County), Denver/Boulder, Boise, the Reno-area industrial center (Storey County, the Tesla Gigafactory).
- **Historical differentiators:** hard-rock mining camps (Butte, Leadville, Virginia City, Wallace ID), railroad towns, uranium.

### Linguistic regions (narrow → general)
- **Western American English (L1 West):** cot–caught merged, Low-Back-Merger Shift.
- **Utah English:** "fill/feel" and "pull/pool" mergers before /l/, *oh my heck*, *fetch*, glottal t ("moun'ain"). It extends into SE Idaho, the Mormon Corridor.
- **Northern tier (MT, northern ID):** Upper-Midwest-like features and some Canadian raising.
- **Hispano Spanish:** the San Luis Valley (Costilla, Conejos CO).
- **Basque:** Boise, Elko and Winnemucca form the largest Basque diaspora in the US.
- **Indigenous languages:** Crow (one of the most vital Plains languages), Blackfeet, Northern Cheyenne, Shoshone and Arapaho (Wind River), Ute, Paiute.
- **Las Vegas:** leveled transplant speech, with large Spanish- and Tagalog-speaking populations.

### Religious views
- **Latter-day Saints core:** Utah (Utah County is the most LDS large county), SE Idaho (Madison and Franklin rank among the highest LDS shares in the US), and western Wyoming (Lincoln, Uinta). This makes R03 (concentration) the highest of any region.
- **Outside the Mormon Corridor, among the least religious areas in the US:** Montana, Wyoming, Nevada and the Colorado mountain towns.
- Catholic in Hispanic areas and Butte (Irish). Lutheran in north-central and eastern Montana. Hutterite colonies (MT). An Evangelical hub in Colorado Springs (Focus on the Family). The "American Redoubt" in North Idaho.

### Environment
- **Rocky Mountains** (Northern, Middle and Southern) and the **Continental Divide**, which separates Atlantic/Gulf drainage from Pacific drainage.
- **Great Basin:** NV and western UT have internal drainage with no outlet to the sea.
- Also the Colorado Plateau (southern UT, western CO), the Wyoming Basin, the Snake River Plain (an irrigated arc), the eastern Great Plains (east of the Front Range), and the Mojave (southern NV).
- **Most federal land in the US:** about 80% of NV, and roughly half to two-thirds of UT, ID and WY.
- **A paradox:** urban corridors (the Wasatch Front, the Front Range, Las Vegas) make UT and NV among the most *urbanized* states, while most counties are frontier (<6 people/sq mi).
- The highest mean elevation (CO). Arid, with heavy snowpack. Wildfire. Prior-appropriation water law. Entirely west of the Mississippi.

### Voting history
- **The most Republican region overall:** WY is the most R state; ID and UT are deep R.
- **Utah's distinctive conservatism:** Trump-skeptical (Evan McMullin took 21% in 2016).
- **Colorado has shifted Democratic:** Denver, Boulder, the suburbs and the ski counties. Resort counties are D islands (Pitkin, San Miguel, Summit CO; Teton WY; Blaine ID).
- **Nevada is a swing state:** Clark and Washoe versus the rural "cow counties".
- **Montana has a split-ticket tradition.** Reservation counties (Glacier, Big Horn) vote D.
- **Historical alignment:** Populist/Free Silver in 1896 (Bryan swept the region) and a libertarian Sagebrush Rebellion streak.

## 6. The Pacific Northwest

**Core states:** WA, OR. (North Idaho shares an affinity; Alaska is scored in Region 7.)

### History
- **Colonial:** Spanish, British and Russian exploration (Cook, 1778; Gray's Columbia River, 1792; Vancouver). Salmon-based Native cultures (Chinook, Coast Salish, Yakama, Nez Perce). A low colonial-settlement score.
- **Founding years:** Lewis and Clark reached the Pacific (Fort Clatsop, 1805). Astoria (1811), the first American settlement on the Pacific coast.
- **Antebellum:** Hudson's Bay Company Fort Vancouver. The **Oregon Trail** (1840s) and Willamette Valley settlement. The Oregon Treaty (1846). Oregon statehood (1859, with Black exclusion laws).
- **Industrial (peak era):** The Northern Pacific Railroad (1883), Seattle as the Klondike gateway (1897), timber and salmon canneries. Boeing founded (1916). Labor radicalism (Seattle General Strike, 1919).
- **Depression:** **Grand Coulee and Bonneville dams** brought cheap hydropower and the Columbia Basin irrigation project.
- **Postwar:** **Hanford** (Manhattan Project; Benton County), the Boeing jet age, Japanese American incarceration.
- **Reagan era:** Timber decline begins. Microsoft moves to the Seattle area (1979). Nike (Beaverton).
- **Tech boom (peak era for King County):** Microsoft, Amazon (1994), Intel's "Silicon Forest" (Hillsboro).
- **Modern:** The Amazon/cloud boom in Seattle. Data centers in the Columbia Basin (Quincy, The Dalles). Struggling timber towns.
- **Projections:** Puget Sound growth continues and the region is a climate refuge west of the Cascades, but faces drought and wildfire to the east and Cascadia subduction-zone earthquake risk.

### Jobs / main unique positions
- **Centers of industry:** software and cloud (King: Microsoft, Amazon), commercial aerospace (Snohomish: Boeing Everett), semiconductors (Washington Co. OR: Intel), athletic apparel (Nike, Columbia).
- **Specializations:** apples and cherries (Yakima, Chelan; WA grows most US apples), hops (the Yakima Valley grows most US hops), wine (Walla Walla, the Willamette Valley), wheat (the Palouse; Whitman), potatoes (the Columbia Basin), timber and lumber (Oregon is the top softwood lumber state; Douglas, Lane, Coos, Grays Harbor), the Seattle-based Alaska fishing fleet, ports (Seattle/Tacoma, Portland), the military (JB Lewis-McChord, Naval Base Kitsap), nuclear cleanup (Hanford).
- **Historical differentiators:** timber mill towns, salmon canneries, aluminum smelters (powered by cheap hydro).

### Linguistic regions (narrow → general)
- **Pacific Northwest English:** close to General American and often perceived as "accentless". Cot–caught merged, pre-velar raising ("bayg" for *bag*), some Canadian raising, and the California-style shift in younger speakers.
- **Chinook Jargon loanwords:** *skookum*, *tillicum*, *saltchuck*, *high muckamuck*.
- **Inland Northwest (Spokane):** more Western/Plains in character.
- **Spanish:** majority-Hispanic counties in the Yakima Valley and Columbia Basin (Adams, Franklin, Yakima, Grant).
- **Asian languages** around Seattle (Chinese, Vietnamese, Tagalog, Korean). **Slavic** communities (Russian and Ukrainian) in Spokane and Portland. Revival of Lushootseed and Sahaptin.
- Soft drink: *pop*.

### Religious views
- **The "None Zone":** the lowest adherence rates and among the highest unaffiliated shares in the US (WA, OR).
- **Catholic** is the largest single group (driven by the Hispanic east side).
- Evangelical and nondenominational churches in rural eastern areas. LDS in eastern OR and WA. Lutheran heritage (Ballard, Skagit). Buddhist and spiritual-but-not-religious populations.
- Historical oddity: Rajneeshpuram (Wasco, 1980s).

### Environment
- **The defining divide is the Cascade crest, "wet side / dry side".** West of the crest: a marine climate with roughly 40–150+ inches of rain (the Olympic temperate rainforest). East of the crest: semi-arid shrub-steppe, under 10 inches in parts of the Columbia Basin.
- **Landforms:** the Coast Range, the Olympics, the Puget Sound lowland (fjord), the Willamette Valley, the Columbia River Gorge, the Columbia Plateau (lava flows), the Palouse (loess hills), the Blue and Klamath mountains, and the SE Oregon high desert / Basin and Range (Harney, Malheur, Lake: vast and nearly empty).
- **Cascade volcanoes:** Rainier, St. Helens (1980), Hood.
- **Urbanization** concentrated along I-5. Oregon's urban growth boundaries are a unique land-use regime. Federal land: about half of OR.

### Voting history
- **Politics follows the Cascade climate divide.** West/urban is strongly D (King and Multnomah are among the most D large counties). East/rural is strongly R.
- **"Greater Idaho":** around a dozen eastern Oregon counties have voted to explore joining Idaho.
- **Vote-by-mail pioneers:** Oregon went all-mail in 1998; Washington followed. Washington uses a top-two primary. Both have a strong ballot-measure culture.
- **Southwest WA timber counties** (Grays Harbor, Pacific, Cowlitz) went from old labor Democrats to Republican from 2016.
- Republican or swing before 1988.

## 7. Non-Contiguous Pacific & West Coast

**Sub-regions:** Pacific West (CA, split NorCal/SoCal) · Outlying Pacific (AK, HI)

### History
- **Colonial (non-English, three distinct systems):**
  - Spanish California: San Diego (1769), 21 missions, presidios (San Francisco, 1776).
  - Russian America: Kodiak (1784), Sitka, Fort Ross CA (1812).
  - The Hawaiian Kingdom, unified by Kamehameha I (1810).
  - California had the most linguistically diverse Native population in North America; Alaska Natives and Native Hawaiians add further layers.
- **Founding years:** Spanish Alta California and the independent Hawaiian Kingdom; little US imprint.
- **Antebellum (peak for NorCal):** Mexican California (1821). **The Gold Rush (1848–55)**. Statehood (1850). Missionaries and sugar in Hawaii (1820+).
- **Industrial:** The Central Pacific railroad (Chinese labor), the Chinese Exclusion Act (1882), the Alaska Purchase (1867), the Klondike and Nome gold rushes, the overthrow (1893) and annexation (1898) of Hawaii, the LA oil and citrus booms.
- **Roaring 20s:** Hollywood, aviation, LA oil.
- **Depression:** Dust Bowl migrants to the Central Valley, the Golden Gate Bridge, the Central Valley Project.
- **Postwar (peak era for SoCal):**
  - Pearl Harbor, WWII defense industry, Japanese American incarceration (Manzanar).
  - Statehood for AK and HI (1959).
  - Freeway suburbs (Orange County, the San Fernando Valley) and aerospace.
  - Silicon Valley's origins (Fairchild, 1957) and Prudhoe Bay's discovery (1968).
- **Reagan era:** Reagan was governor of California. Prop 13 (1978). The defense buildup, then the post-Cold-War aerospace bust. The Trans-Alaska Pipeline (1977) and the Permanent Fund dividend (1982). Exxon Valdez (1989).
- **Tech boom (peak era for the Bay Area):** dot-com (Santa Clara, San Mateo, San Francisco).
- **Modern:** Big Tech and the AI boom in San Francisco. The housing crisis and out-migration. Catastrophic wildfires (Paradise 2018; Lahaina, Maui 2023; Los Angeles, January 2025). Declining Alaska oil output.
- **Projections:**
  - California: flat to slow growth, shifting from the coast inland (the Inland Empire, the Central Valley).
  - Alaska and Hawaii: stagnant or declining.
  - Climate exposure: Sierra snowpack and water, sea level, and Arctic warming that is forcing Alaska village relocations.

### Jobs / main unique positions
- **California centers of industry:** tech, semiconductors and AI (Santa Clara, San Mateo, SF), entertainment (LA), the busiest US port complex (LA/Long Beach) and Inland Empire warehousing, the top US agricultural counties by value (Fresno, Tulare, Kern, Monterey "Salad Bowl"), wine (Napa, Sonoma), Kern oil, defense (the Navy in San Diego), biotech (Bay Area, San Diego), state government (Sacramento).
- **Alaska:** North Slope oil, commercial fishing (Bristol Bay sockeye; Aleutians West/Dutch Harbor, top US port by volume), military (JBER, Fort Wainwright, Eielson), mining (Red Dog zinc in Northwest Arctic; gold), tourism, a subsistence economy.
- **Hawaii:** tourism (Maui, Honolulu), military (Indo-Pacific Command, Pearl Harbor), Kona coffee, astronomy (Maunakea).
- **Historical differentiators:** Gold Rush mining (Sierra foothills), Humboldt timber, SoCal aerospace, sugar and pineapple (HI; the last sugar plantation closed in 2016), salmon canneries (AK), Alaska gold camps.

### Linguistic regions (narrow → general)
- **California English:** the California Vowel Shift (lowered TRAP/DRESS, fronted /u/), *dude*, *hella* (NorCal), "*the* 405" (SoCal freeway naming).
- **California is the most multilingual area in the US:**
  - Spanish: Chicano English in East LA and the Central Valley; Imperial County is mostly Spanish-speaking at home.
  - Asian and other languages: Chinese (SF), Korean, Tagalog and Armenian (LA/Glendale), Vietnamese (Little Saigon), Punjabi (Yuba/Sutter).
- **Central Valley:** "Okie" Southern-influenced heritage speech (the Bakersfield sound).
- **Alaska:** a Western base with Upper-Midwest/Canadian traits. **Twenty Alaska Native languages are official.** Central Yupʼik is the most spoken; in the Kusilvak Census Area most residents speak Yupʼik at home. Also Iñupiaq, Unangax̂ (Aleut), Tlingit and Gwichʼin.
- **Hawaii:** **Hawaiʻi Creole English ("Pidgin")**, recognized by the Census as a language in 2015. ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi is an official state language (with revitalization and a native-speaker community on Niʻihau). Also Ilocano, Tagalog and Japanese.

### Religious views
- **California:**
  - Catholic is the largest group (Hispanic, Filipino).
  - Large unaffiliated share, especially in the Bay Area.
  - The SoCal megachurch movement (Calvary Chapel, Saddleback) and Central Valley evangelicals.
  - The second-largest Jewish community (LA), Armenian Apostolic (Glendale), the largest Sikh community (Yuba/Sutter), large Muslim, Hindu and Buddhist populations.
- **Alaska:**
  - Low adherence overall.
  - Russian Orthodox legacy in Native villages (the Aleutians, Kodiak, the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta) and Moravian (Bethel).
  - Evangelical in Mat-Su; LDS.
- **Hawaii:** the highest Buddhist share of any state, Catholic (Filipino), LDS (Lāʻie, BYU-Hawaii), and Native Hawaiian practice.

### Environment
- **California:**
  - Mountains and valleys: the Coast Ranges, the Central Valley, the Sierra Nevada (Mt. Whitney), the Transverse and Peninsular ranges, the Klamath Mountains.
  - Deserts and basins: the Mojave and Colorado deserts, Death Valley (the lowest point in North America), the Modoc Plateau.
  - The San Andreas Fault. A Mediterranean climate. A NorCal/SoCal divide at the Tehachapis and a coast-versus-inland gradient.
  - From Los Angeles County (about 10M people) to Alpine County (about 1,200).
- **Alaska:**
  - Boroughs plus Unorganized Borough census areas, some larger than most states (North Slope).
  - Arctic tundra and permafrost, the Brooks and Alaska ranges (Denali), Aleutian volcanoes, Inside Passage rainforest.
  - Road-less communities (Juneau has no road access). Lowest density of any state. About 60% federal land.
- **Hawaii:**
  - Volcanic islands and active lava (Hawaiʻi County).
  - Windward/leeward rain shadows: Waiʻaleʻale is among the wettest spots on Earth, with dry leeward coasts.
  - 5 county-equivalents, including Kalawao, the former Hansen's disease settlement and the smallest county by population after Loving TX.

### Voting history
- **California:** Republican or swing through the 1980s (Nixon, Reagan), Democratic since 1992. The coast is strongly D; the Central Valley, the Inland Empire fringe and the far north are R. Orange County turned D in 2016–2020. Latino-heavy counties shifted right in 2024.
- **Alaska:** Republican at the presidential level since 1968, with a strong independent streak. Ranked-choice voting was adopted in 2020, used from 2022, and survived a 2024 repeal attempt. Bush Alaska Native regions lean D. **Results are reported by house district, not by borough.**
- **Hawaii:** strongly Democratic since statehood, except the 1972 and 1984 landslides.

## Cross-region notes for scoring

- **History weighting differs by region.** The added Postwar era (H06b) is essential for regions 2, 4, 5 and 7. Without it, those regions' signature growth disappears from the History category.
- **Colonial scoring must count Spanish, French and Russian settlement.** Otherwise regions 3, 4 and 7 are wrongly scored as having no colonial layer.
- **Per-area metrics behave very differently in the West** (huge counties, mostly federal land). Check C1 (size bias) will flag many Mountain West and Alaska counties; use per-capita forms there.
- **Within-region divides are often larger than between-region differences:** the Cascade crest, the 100th meridian, coastal vs Upland South, NorCal vs SoCal, Wasatch Front vs rural Nevada. The `regional_typicality` score exists to surface these.
