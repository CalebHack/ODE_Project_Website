# US County Scoring System: Parameter Definitions

This document defines what gets scored, how each number is computed, where the data comes from, and how results are verified. The machine-readable version is [`parameters.json`](parameters.json). The region-by-region description of how each category differs is in [`REGIONAL_PROFILES.md`](REGIONAL_PROFILES.md).

## 0. Decisions made on the brief

| Brief said | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "5 central categories" | 6 scored categories plus 1 verification step (7 items total) | The list has seven items. Item 7 (per capita / per area check) is a test applied to the other six, not a category of its own. |
| "Great Depression and progressive era" after "Roaring 20s" | Progressive reforms (1896–1917) are scored inside the Industrial era; the Depression era covers 1929–1941 and the New Deal | The Progressive Era happened before the 1920s. Keeping the label but fixing the dates avoids double counting. |
| Eras jump from the Depression to Reagan | Added **H06b Postwar boom & Civil Rights (1942–1980)**. Set its weight to 0 to remove it. | Without it, WWII industry, the Interstates, suburbs, Sun Belt growth, the space program and the Civil Rights movement have nowhere to go. That would leave the Southeast, Southwest and California badly under-scored. |
| "per sq foot" | Scored per **square mile**; per square foot is available as a display unit | A typical county has about 0.0001 residents per square foot, which is unreadable. 1 sq mi = 27,878,400 sq ft, so converting is exact. |
| Region list omits DC; Idaho and Alaska appear in two regions | DC goes to South Atlantic. Idaho goes to the Mountain West and Alaska to Region 7, one region each. North Idaho carries a Pacific Northwest *secondary affinity* tag. | Every county needs exactly one home region for regional comparisons. |

## 1. Unit of analysis

- **County-equivalents, 2023 Census vintage: 3,144 units.** This covers counties, Louisiana parishes, Alaska boroughs and census areas, Virginia's independent cities, Connecticut's 9 planning regions (which replaced its 8 counties in 2022) and DC.
- The full list with region assignments is in [`data/county_roster.csv`](data/county_roster.csv), generated by `npm run build:roster`.
- Units per region: Northeast 218 · Southeast 1,091 · Midwest 1,055 · Southwest 379 · Mountain West 233 · Pacific NW 75 · Pacific/Non-contiguous 93.
- Roster geography (centroid, area, Mississippi side) is approximate, taken from 1:10m boundaries. Replace it with the Census Gazetteer before scoring. Connecticut planning regions and Alaska's Chugach and Copper River areas are marked `pending_gazetteer`.

## 2. Scoring conventions

| Level | Range | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-parameter | 0–10 | By default 10 × the county's national percentile, so 5.0 is the median US county. |
| Signed sub-parameter | −10 to +10 | 0 is the national reference point. Used for partisan lean and trend. |
| Categorical sub-parameter | label + 0–10 | A label (e.g. "Inland North", "Catholic", "RUCC 7") stored next to an intensity score. |
| Category | 0–100 | 10 × weighted mean of its sub-parameters. Signed parameters enter as their absolute value, so the category measures how *distinctive* a county is, not which direction it leans. |
| Composite | 0–100 | Weighted mean of the six categories (equal weights by default). It is an index of distinctiveness, not a ranking of better or worse places. |
| Regional typicality | 0–1 | Cosine similarity of the county's full sub-parameter vector to its region's mean vector. |

**Small-county shrinkage.** For any rate in a county under 5,000 residents, the rate is pulled toward the state mean before ranking: `x_adj = (n·x + 2000·μ_state) / (n + 2000)`. Without this, a county of 64 people (Loving County, TX) would dominate every per-capita ranking.

## 3. Category definitions

### Category 1: History (weight 1)

Each era gets an **imprint score** (0–10), meaning how much that era shaped the county as it exists today.

```
era_score = 10 × (0.40·G + 0.25·B + 0.20·L + 0.15·V)       (each term a 0–1 national percentile)
  G = share of the county's cumulative population gains that happened in the era
  B = share of surviving housing stock built in the era          (ACS B25034)
  L = NRHP listings with period of significance in the era,
      mean of per-100-sq-mi and per-10k-resident percentiles
  V = curated event salience: 0, 0.5 or 1 (battlefield, capital, dam, boom, disaster, etc.)

Pre-1790 eras (no census): 0.45·Settlement + 0.35·L + 0.20·V
  Settlement = 1 if any colonial settlement existed (English, Spanish, French, Dutch, Swedish, Russian),
               0.5 if claimed frontier, 0 otherwise
```

| ID | Era | Years | Census window used for G |
|---|---|---|---|
| H01 | Colonial America (all colonial powers) | 1565–1775 | none (settlement rule) |
| H02 | America's founding years | 1776–1815 | 1790–1810 |
| H03 | Pre-Civil War through Civil War | 1816–1865 | 1810–1860 |
| H04 | Industrial period (incl. Progressive reforms) | 1866–1919 | 1860–1920 |
| H05 | Roaring 20s | 1920–1929 | 1920–1930 |
| H06 | Great Depression & New Deal | 1929–1941 | 1930–1940 |
| H06b | Postwar boom & Civil Rights *(added)* | 1942–1980 | 1940–1980 |
| H07 | Reagan era | 1981–1992 | 1980–1990 |
| H08 | Tech boom | 1993–2007 | 1990–2000 + ½(2000–2010) |
| H09 | Modern | 2008–present | ½(2000–2010) + 2010–2020 + 2020–latest estimate |
| H10 | Projections / future | 2025–2050 | Projected growth (0.6), inverted climate risk (0.25), inverted water stress (0.15) |

Derived fields: **dominant era** (the highest of H01–H09) and **history depth** (the number of eras scoring 6 or more). A colonial Virginia county might have a depth of 8; a Las Vegas-era Nevada county might have 2.

### Category 2: Jobs / main unique positions

| ID | Parameter | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| J01 | **Center of industry** | The county's share of *national* employment in its leading 3-digit NAICS industry (only industries with 500+ local jobs). Score = percentile of log(share). Label = industry. This captures national hubs such as Santa Clara (semiconductors), Elkhart IN (RVs), Whitfield GA (carpets) and Campbell WY (coal). |
| J02 | **Specialization** | 0.6 × percentile of the maximum location quotient + 0.4 × percentile of the Herfindahl concentration across sectors. LQ = (local industry share) / (national industry share), so an LQ of 5 means 5× the national concentration. Also stores the USDA ERS economic-type label. |
| J03 | **Historical differentiators** | Legacy industry index covering coal, metal mining, steel, textiles, autos, timber/paper, fishing, oil & gas, rail, tobacco, cotton, ranching, shipbuilding and defense. `score = 10 × max(0.5·magnitude + 0.5·magnitude·persistence)`, where magnitude = percentile of the industry's peak historical LQ and persistence = current LQ / peak LQ, capped at 1. A high score with low persistence marks a lost industry that still defines the place (e.g. Youngstown steel). |

### Category 3: Linguistic regions (narrowest → general)

Every county gets labels at four levels:

| Level | Scope | Example (Allegheny County, PA) |
|---|---|---|
| L4 | Local features | /aw/ monophthong ("dahntahn"), "yinz", "needs washed", "n'at" |
| L3 | Dialect area | Western Pennsylvania |
| L2 | Dialect region (ANAE) | Midland |
| L1 | Macro region | North / **Midland** / South / West / Non-contiguous |

| ID | Parameter | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| L01 | Dialect assignment | L1–L3 labels from the Atlas of North American English. Counties between surveyed cities take the nearest ANAE community in the same region. Intensity = number of isogloss bundles (dialect boundary lines) crossing or enclosing the county. |
| L02 | Marked features | Weighted count of features that differ from General American (the inventory of 22 features is in `parameters.json`; e.g. non-rhoticity 2, Northern Cities Shift 2, pin–pen merger 1, y'all 1, yinz/youse/you'uns 2). Normalized to 0–10. |
| L03 | Non-English presence | Percentile of the population speaking a language other than English at home (ACS C16001). Label = top language. |
| L04 | Heritage / contact variety | Curated 0–10. 10 = spoken by a substantial share (Gullah, Louisiana French, Pennsylvania Dutch, Hawaiʻi Creole, Navajo, Yupʼik, New Mexican Spanish); 5 = heritage or revival presence only. |

### Category 4: Religious views (% who identify as…)

**Data limitation:** no survey measures self-identification at county level. The US Religion Census counts congregation *adherents*, which is a different measure from self-identification. The system therefore **estimates** self-identification by raking:

1. Start with state self-identification shares (Pew RLS 2023–24, PRRI).
2. Multiply each tradition by (county adherence rate ÷ state adherence rate) for that tradition.
3. Renormalize to 100%.

Both the estimated self-identification share and the raw adherence figure are stored.

| ID | Parameter | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| R01 | % by tradition | Evangelical Protestant, Mainline Protestant, Black Protestant, Catholic, Latter-day Saints, Orthodox, Jewish, Muslim, Hindu/Buddhist/other Eastern, Other, Unaffiliated. Label = largest. |
| R02 | Religiosity | Percentile of total adherents per 1,000 residents. |
| R03 | Concentration | Percentile of the Herfindahl index of tradition shares; high means one faith dominates (e.g. Utah County). |
| R04 | Distinctiveness | Dissimilarity from the national mix: ½ Σ \|county share − US share\|. |

### Category 5: Environment

| ID | Parameter | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| E01 | Rural vs urban | 0.5 × (10 − 1.25·(RUCC − 1)) + 0.5 × percentile of log density. 10 = most urban. |
| E02 | Mountains & divides | 0.5 × relief percentile + 0.5 × elevation percentile. Labels: physiographic province, Continental Divide side, Cascade crest side, Appalachian Regional Commission member. |
| E03 | Climate & water | Aridity score (10 = most arid). Labels: Köppen class, desert (Mojave, Sonoran, Chihuahuan, Great Basin), river basin, Great Basin internal-drainage flag. |
| E04 | Positional factors | Labels: **east/west/straddles the Mississippi** (already in the roster), east/west of the 100th meridian, coastline type, EPA ecoregion. Score = federal land share. |
| E05 | Hazard exposure | FEMA National Risk Index expected-annual-loss percentile. Label = top hazard. |

### Category 6: Voting history

| ID | Parameter | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| V01 | Partisan lean (signed) | (County R two-party margin − national margin), weighted 2016/2020/2024 at 0.25/0.35/0.40. Score = margin ÷ 5, capped at ±10. +10 means R+50 or more relative to the nation. |
| V02 | Trend (signed) | Regression slope of relative margin per cycle, 2000–2024, ÷ 1.5, capped at ±10. |
| V03 | Volatility | Percentile of the standard deviation of cycle-to-cycle swing. |
| V04 | Turnout | Percentile of votes ÷ citizen voting-age population, 2020–24 average. |
| V05 | Historical alignment | Winning party in 1896, 1932, 1964, 1980, 2008 and 2024 (e.g. `DDDRDR`). Score = switches ÷ 5 × 10. |

Data caveats: **Alaska reports presidential results by state house district, not by borough.** Precinct results must be allocated to boroughs before scoring. New England reports by town, so those results must be aggregated up.

## 4. Category 7: Final verification (per capita and per area)

Every count-based input is computed as **raw**, **per 1,000 residents** and **per square mile**, and passes six checks before a score is released:

| Check | Test | Action |
|---|---|---|
| C1 Size bias | Spearman ρ of each parameter vs log(population) and log(area) | If \|ρ\| > 0.6, the parameter is just measuring size. Switch to the per-capita or per-area form. |
| C2 Small numbers | Rates in counties under 5k residents | Apply shrinkage; confidence capped at B. |
| C3 Rank agreement | Raw vs per-capita vs per-area ranks | Flag "scale-driven" (top decile raw, below median per capita) or "small-but-intense" (the reverse). |
| C4 Regional coherence | z-score within the region | \|z\| > 3 goes to manual review. |
| C5 Known answers | Benchmark counties (table below) | A benchmark outside its expected band blocks release of that category. |
| C6 Temporal stability | Re-score with the prior data vintage | A move of more than 20 points without a documented cause is investigated. |

**Benchmark counties (C5).** These are well-documented cases the scoring must reproduce:

| County | Expected result |
|---|---|
| Utah County, UT | R01 label = Latter-day Saints; R03 ≥ 9 |
| Wayne County, MI | J03 high (auto legacy); V01 strongly negative |
| Allegheny County, PA | L3 = Western PA; L02 high (/aw/ monophthong, yinz) |
| Whitfield County, GA | J01 label = carpet/textile mills; J02 ≥ 9 |
| Elkhart County, IN | J01 label = RV / transportation equipment; J02 ≥ 9 |
| Santa Clara County, CA | J01 = semiconductors / computers; H08 high |
| Campbell County, WY | J01 = coal mining; J02 ≥ 9 |
| Los Alamos County, NM | J01 = scientific R&D; H06b high |
| Harlan County, KY | J03 high, persistence low (coal) |
| Kusilvak Census Area, AK | L04 = 10 (Yupʼik); E01 near 0 |
| Starr County, TX | L03 ≥ 9 (Spanish); V02 strongly positive |
| New York County, NY | E01 = 10; per-area metrics extreme (C3 flag expected) |
| Loving County, TX | Smallest population; shrinkage must apply (C2) |
| St. Johns County, FL | H01 high (St. Augustine 1565) |
| Santa Fe County, NM | H01 high (1610), L04 ≥ 5 (New Mexican Spanish) |
| Acadia Parish, LA | L04 high (Louisiana French); R01 = Catholic |
| Holmes County, OH | L04 = 10 (Pennsylvania Dutch); R01 shows a large Amish/Mennonite share |
| Plaquemines Parish, LA | mississippi_side = straddles |

**Confidence grade per county:** A = all inputs county-level and current · B = some inputs shrunk, interpolated or derived from state data · C = mostly curated or regional default.

## 5. Data pipeline (next build steps)

1. Swap roster geography for the Census Gazetteer (land area, internal point).
2. Load sources per category (listed in `parameters.json` → `sources`).
3. Compute raw metrics → apply shrinkage → percentiles → sub-parameter scores.
4. Run checks C1–C6 and assign confidence grades.
5. Compute category, composite and regional-typicality scores and write them into the roster's empty columns.
